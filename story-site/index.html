At a glance

The study in four results.

Can I give a trained model a new habit by writing a few of its numbers directly, instead of training it again? I tried it on my own 125M legal question-answering model. The habit: saying “not stated in the context” when the context in the prompt doesn’t hold the answer.

Finding 01↓

It learns to say “not stated.”

From 0% to 72.4% on missing-answer questions in the frozen exam.
Finding 02↓

It forgets almost nothing.

+0.011% on held-out text, against +5.86% and +8.87% for fine-tuning.
Finding 03↓

The edit changed3 vectors in layer 7’s MLP2,304 numbersnothing else

The base model was only QA fine-tuned: no RAG, no RAFT, no DPO, no RLAIF. Each question comes with its context written into the prompt. There is no retrieval step.

How it works

One neuron, two eyes and a hand.

The neuron fires only when both eyes say yes. When it fires, the hand pushes the reply toward “not stated in the context”. Everything else in the model stays exactly as it was.

  • built from edit_dev only
  • pre-registered before building
  • 3 random controls
  • 3 structured controls
  • originals hash-locked
Six steps, from symptom to verdict

The whole edit, in two lines

activation = SiLU(gate·x) × (up·x)
output += activation × down x̂ is the input to layer 7’s MLP (768 numbers). The SwiGLU block already computes this product, so no new mechanism is added; if either factor is near zero, the neuron writes almost nothing.

How I measured the keyword effect

I built a test set where “answer missing” and “answer present” contexts share the same amount of wording with the question. Before matching, word overlap alone separates them almost perfectly (AUROC 0.966). After matching it can’t (0.502, a coin flip).

D is how much more often the model abstains when the answer is missing than when it is present. A D of 0.72 that falls to 0.12 once overlap is matched means most of the signal was overlap.

Limits

What this doesn’t prove.

The limitations and answers below are the ones written in the final report (docs/20).

  • One model, one behaviour.Abstention is a fixed phrase, which makes it a favourable case for constructed edits.
  • Small samples.One random-search run, three editor runs, a single recursion iteration.
  • The editors were Claude-family agentsworking from a written brief. Results may differ for other editors or briefs.
  • The exam’s missing-answer condition always has 2 context passages.No arm was selected on that shortcut, but the exam still carries it.
  • Retention and copy were measured on held-out text,not on the exam. The two are separate by design.
  • Costs that were not targeted.False abstention rose in every arm (+0.06 to +0.16 on the exam), and closed-book F1 fell by 0.05–0.11.

Can an AI editor author useful weight edits?

Yes in this action space: all three passed every guardrail. No as evidence that the editor matters: random search did too.

Does it beat fine-tuning?

Not on raw abstention (0.92–0.93 against 0.72–0.91). Yes on abstention against forgetting: no fine-tuning arm stays inside the budget.

Does self-recursion compound?

One step did, at a cost to F1 that just exceeded the approved budget.
