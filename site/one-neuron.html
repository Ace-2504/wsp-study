Weight Programming.

I taught a legal AI model to say "I don't know" by programming its weights directly, with no retraining.

My legal question-answering model never admitted when an answer wasn't in its documents. It just made something up. So I rewired a single neuron, out of 36,864, and tested the result on 2,000 questions it had never seen.

For engineers

Model: Ace-2504/slm-125m-sft, LLaMA-style, 12 layers, hidden size 768, 12 heads, SwiGLU MLP with 3,072 neurons per layer, vocabulary 16,384. Its only post-training is one full QA supervised fine-tune of the pretrained slm-125m-e4 on 15k grounded QA pairs: no RAFT, DPO or RLAIF stage, and no retriever. The weight edit is the only thing added on top.

The edit: block index 6 (the 7th block), neuron 1880. Its gate row becomes a linear probe for "answer absent" read at the last prompt token. Its up row becomes a ridge-regression "reply is starting" detector. Its down column writes a 768-number direction that raises the chance of "not stated in the context". The neuron's output is SiLU(gate·x)·(up·x), so it is large only when both checks are on. That is the AND.

Honest detail: the probe and detector were fitted with regression, and the written direction was found by a small gradient optimisation. No full training run, but not purely hand-typed numbers either.

Testing: every experiment was pre-registered. Final-test numbers are paired against the original model, with bootstrap 95% CIs (abstention +0.724 [0.684, 0.762]). Full reports: the edit, the keyword test, the final test.

How I changed it

One worker, rewired. Four steps.

Each of the model's 12 layers holds 3,072 neurons. Think of each one as a worker on an assembly line. As the model reads, it carries a "thought": 768 numbers that describe what it understands so far. Every worker reads that thought through two eyes, decides how hard to react, and writes back with one hand.

Gate row · eye 1"Should I switch on?"768 numbers that score the thought
Up row · eye 2"How much?"768 numbers, a second score
Down column · hand"What do I write?"768 numbers added back to the thought

reaction = squash(gate score) × up score · if either eye reads zero, the worker stays quiet. That multiplication is the AND.

what changed: worker 1880 in layer 7 · 3 × 768 = 2,304 numbers out of 125.8 million (0.002%) · every other number is identical

  1. 1

    Pick the worker nobody needs

    I measured how much each of the layer's 3,072 workers contributes on ordinary text. Worker 1880 contributed the least, so giving it a new job costs the least.

  2. 2

    Eye 1: does the answer look missing?

    I recorded the model's thought at the end of thousands of training questions, labelled each one "answer missing" or "answer present", and fitted a logistic-regression detector to tell them apart. Scaled up, it works like a switch, not a dimmer. The catch: it mostly learned keyword overlap, not real understanding.

  3. 3

    Eye 2: is the reply about to start?

    My first version had only eye 1. It also fired in the middle of ordinary text and made SEC filings 1.72% worse. A second, ridge-regression detector reads about 1 at the moment a reply starts and about 0 everywhere else. Multiplied together, forgetting fell to 0.011%.

  4. 4

    The hand: write "not stated in the context"

    With the whole model frozen, I optimised one 768-number nudge that makes "not stated in the context" most likely when it is added at the reply start. Only those 768 numbers moved. I capped its size, then divided it by the worker's usual firing strength.

Before any test, an automatic safety gate checked the size of the change: at most 25% to any one weight matrix (this edit moved layer 7's gate matrix by about 20%) and at most 5% to the whole model. Then came held-out questions, and last, the untouched 2,000-question exam.

What I learned

It worked. Then I caught it cheating.

0% → 72%

One neuron can teach a new habit

Rewiring one neuron got the model to say "not stated in the context" on 72% of missing-answer questions, and it forgot almost nothing else. Fine-tuning reached about 93% but got 6–9% worse at ordinary text.

0.72 → 0.12

But it mostly matches keywords

When I deleted the answer sentence but kept the surrounding words, its ability to tell answerable from unanswerable fell from 0.72 to 0.12. It checks whether the question's words appear, not whether the answer does.

3 holes

What I'm fixing next

My fine-tuning comparison wasn't tuned to avoid forgetting, my random-search control is one run, and the edit wrongly refuses 6% of answerable questions. Part 2 gives fine-tuning a fair fight.

One neuron can teach a model a new habit without forgetting, but it can only use what the model already understands.
